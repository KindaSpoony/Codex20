Overview: The OSINT Investigative Playbook (provided as OSINT_Playbook.yaml) is a comprehensive guide for conducting open-source intelligence investigations with intelligence-community-level rigor. It is designed for a dual audience:
	•	Non-technical users (analysts, decision-makers) get a clear blueprint of the investigative process, ensuring transparency and trust in the results.
	•	Technical users (developers, OSINT tool integrators) get a structured workflow that can be implemented or automated.

This playbook’s purpose is to standardize OSINT workflows so that collection, analysis, and reporting of publicly available information meet the high standards of national security and law enforcement intelligence . By following this playbook, teams can produce actionable insights from current events while adhering to formal tradecraft principles of objectivity, source validation, and ethical conduct.

Installation & Usage: The playbook is delivered as a YAML file which can be used directly in the Codex20 OSINT analyst platform or referenced as documentation for manual analysis. To use it:
	•	For Automated Systems: Import or parse OSINT_Playbook.yaml in your OSINT toolkit. The structured stages and rules can guide an autonomous agent’s behavior. For example, the agent will follow the stages (from Data Collection to Final Reporting) in order, apply source tier weighting for credibility, and trigger escalations if certain threat conditions are met. Ensure any external dependencies (e.g. access to web search APIs, databases, or OSINT tools) are configured before running the playbook.
	•	For Human Analysts: Treat the playbook as a procedural checklist. Start at the Data Collection stage and progress step-by-step. The YAML format is human-readable, but for convenience this documentation explains each part in plain language. Analysts can thus use the playbook to plan and track their investigation, ensuring no critical step is overlooked.

No special installation is required beyond having the YAML file and a suitable text editor or OSINT platform. If using the Codex20 platform, simply loading the playbook will set up the investigative workflow according to its rules. The playbook is modular, so specific sections (e.g. Source Evaluation) could be run as standalone checks within a larger process if needed.

Playbook Structure: The OSINT Playbook is organized into clear sections that mirror an intelligence investigation lifecycle:
	•	Mission Types: Defines the scope of investigations the playbook can handle. Examples include Identity Verification, Digital Footprint Mapping, Threat Pattern Detection, Credibility Scoring, and Chronology Validation. Each mission type is described with its goal. (For instance, Digital footprint mapping focuses on gathering a subject’s online presence across profiles, posts, and public records.) This helps users quickly understand if the playbook covers their analytic question and guides the selection of appropriate methods.
	•	Analysis Plan (Stages): Outlines a step-by-step workflow divided into stages:
	1.	Data Collection: Define the investigation scope and sub-objectives, generate initial hypotheses about sources, gather data from diverse OSINT sources (web search, news archives, public records, social media, academic journals, etc.), all while respecting ethical constraints and logging sources  .
	2.	Source Evaluation: Assess and document the credibility of each source (using criteria like authority, bias, timeliness), categorize sources into reliability tiers 1–3, and cross-verify key facts  . In this stage, the playbook instills a habit of corroborating information — if a critical claim comes from only one source, analysts are prompted to seek independent confirmation .
	3.	Threat Analysis: Examine the collected data for indicators of threats or malicious activity (e.g. disinformation patterns, cyber-attack hints, extremist rhetoric). This stage uses intelligence community models to identify potential threat actors or campaigns and flags high-risk findings for escalation . The playbook emphasizes remaining objective here: even if content is inflammatory, the analyst/agent notes it as potential manipulation rather than getting swayed .
	4.	Temporal Validation: Validate timelines and chronological order of events. Ensure dates and sequences make sense and check facts against known historical context . This stage catches anachronisms or inconsistencies in how the story is unfolding.
	5.	Triangulation and Scoring: Corroborate each key finding with multiple independent sources and assign credibility-based scores  . The rule of thumb in the playbook is to have at least two credible (Tier 1 or 2) sources for any critical piece of information . Conflicting accounts are noted and investigated further rather than ignored. The information is then given a preliminary confidence level (High/Medium/Low) based on evidence consistency and source quality.
	6.	Final Reporting: Synthesize the verified findings into a clear, structured intelligence report. This includes citations for every key fact, an objective summary of findings, and notes on any unresolved conflicts . The playbook stresses a neutral, fact-based tone — even if source material was biased or sensational, the final report should remain professional and avoid unwarranted speculation . Privacy and ethical considerations are respected by omitting irrelevant personal details and clearly labeling unverified information .
	•	Source Tiers: The playbook defines three tiers of source reliability:
	•	Tier 1 – High-credibility sources (e.g. official government data, verified records, major established news outlets). These are considered the most authoritative and are weighted heavily in analysis .
	•	Tier 2 – Moderate-credibility sources (e.g. reputable industry publications, well-sourced Wikipedia articles, local news). Useful but may have some bias or gaps; information here should be cross-checked .
	•	Tier 3 – Low-credibility or unverified sources (e.g. social media posts, personal blogs without citations, forums). These can provide leads and context but are not trusted for confirming facts without higher-tier corroboration .
Each tier has an associated credibility weight (1.0, 0.7, 0.3 respectively in the YAML) that the playbook or system uses to quantitatively score confidence. In practice, an analyst using the playbook will prioritize confirming critical facts via Tier 1 or 2 sources before including them in the final report.
	•	Triangulation Rules: This section sets the requirements for considering a finding as verified. By default, it requires a minimum of two independent sources for any important fact . If only one source is available, the information is flagged as unverified and prompts further investigation . The playbook also gives guidance on resolving conflicts: if two sources disagree, analysts should collect additional evidence and document the discrepancy rather than just choosing one side .
	•	Escalation Rules: Not all OSINT findings are equal in impact. The playbook defines threat level triggers:
	•	High Threat: e.g. credible threats to life, signs of imminent harm, or large-scale disinformation that could cause panic. When these conditions are detected, the playbook dictates an immediate halt of autonomous operations and escalation to human oversight . All evidence is handed to a human analyst team for urgent review.
	•	Medium Threat: ambiguous or concerning content that isn’t an immediate danger (e.g. potential misinformation or ethically sensitive material). The system/analyst flags these for internal review and continues analysis with extra caution .
	•	Low Threat: routine findings with no significant risk indicators. Normal autonomous or standard analysis proceeds with no special actions beyond standard monitoring .
These rules ensure operational scalability and safety – the playbook can be used in autonomous workflows, but it knows when to involve human experts for critical judgment calls.
	•	Confidence Scoring: Finally, the playbook describes how to express the overall confidence in each analytic conclusion:
	•	High Confidence: Well-supported by multiple independent high-tier sources, no credible contradictions, and up-to-date evidence  .
	•	Medium Confidence: Supported by at least one strong source or a few moderate ones, but either not thoroughly corroborated or some minor inconsistencies remain .
	•	Low Confidence: Relies on a single source or mostly low-tier sources, or there are significant unresolved conflicts or doubts about authenticity .
Each conclusion in the final report should be tagged with one of these confidence levels, giving readers a quick sense of how much caution to apply. The playbook’s criteria for each level make sure this grading is done consistently and transparently (e.g., the rationale notes if something is based on Tier 3 rumors, it gets Low confidence by default ).

Contribution Model: The OSINT Investigative Playbook is intended to be a living document. Contributions from the community or team are welcome, provided they uphold the playbook’s integrity and clarity. Key guidelines for contributing include:
	•	Maintain Tradecraft Standards: Any changes or additions should align with real-world intelligence tradecraft principles. This means preserving objectivity (no politicized or biased language), requiring evidence for new guidelines, and ensuring any new method is vetted. For example, if you add a new structured analytic technique or a new source tier criterion, it should be something already recognized in analytic communities or supported by reputable references.
	•	Clarity for Dual Audience: Contributions should be written in clear language accessible to non-technical users, while still providing enough detail for technical implementation. Avoid jargon where possible, or include a brief explanation if a technical term is necessary. Remember that a diverse audience (from intelligence officers to software engineers) will read these docs.
	•	Modular Design: The documentation and playbook are structured for modularity – each section (roles, heuristics, tools, etc.) is relatively self-contained. Contributors should follow this structure, placing information in the appropriate document/section. For instance, content about how analysts coordinate should go into AGENTS.md (not the README), and any new OSINT tool recommendations should go into tool.md under the correct category. This keeps the project organized.
	•	Contribution Process: The project uses a typical GitHub workflow. Contributors can fork the repository, make changes (with clear commit messages), and submit a Pull Request. Each PR will be reviewed by maintainers for accuracy and coherence. It’s recommended to discuss significant changes by opening an Issue first, especially for major additions like a new mission type or a change in escalation logic.
	•	Citation and Integrity: If you include factual statements or best practices drawn from external sources (e.g. an academic paper on OSINT or a government guideline), please cite them in our standard format (as footnote-style references 【】). This not only gives credit but also allows others to verify and learn more. All information in the playbook should be traceable to some authoritative source or established best practice, echoing the playbook’s own emphasis on source reliability.
	•	Testing and Feedback: For any functional changes (like altering how a stage is structured for an automated agent), please provide tests or sample scenarios if possible. For example, if a new tool integration is suggested, show how it fits into the workflow. The goal is to ensure that contributions do not break existing use-cases and indeed improve the investigative outcome or efficiency.
	•	Respect Ethical Guidelines: Contributors must ensure that suggested tools or methods comply with legal and ethical standards. We will not accept contributions that encourage unlawful access to information or violate privacy rights. The playbook is about open-source intelligence, so all techniques should revolve around information that is publicly or legally obtainable. This echoes the playbook’s own directive to adhere to ethical constraints during collection .

By following these guidelines, the community can expand and refine the OSINT Playbook while preserving the high standards that make it trustworthy. Ultimately, this project aims to provide a scalable, reliable framework for OSINT that can be adopted from small team investigations up to large intelligence operations, continually improved by its users.
## Deployment
The `pages/api` directory contains serverless functions compatible with Vercel. After installing dependencies with `npm install`, run `npm run build` to generate the `.next` output. Deploy the repository to Vercel and set the `OPENAI_API_KEY` environment variable.
